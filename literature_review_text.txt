Skin Cancer
Skin cancer is one of the most common cancers in the world, presenting a major public health concern. It can be classified as melanoma, a particularly dangerous type of cancer that accounts for the majority of deaths due to skin cancer, and non-melanoma skin cancers like basal cell carcinoma (BCC) and squamous cell carcinoma (SCC). The key to the survival of the patient lies in early diagnosis. If caught early, the five-year survival rate of melanoma is over 99%; however, this greatly decreases if the cancer has metastasized prior to diagnosis.The traditional method used to detect skin cancer through clinical practice is predominantly based on the physical examination carried out by a dermatologist, who may use the aid of dermoscopy if necessary. In the event that the lesion looks suspicious, a physical biopsy is conducted. Although this approach works quite well, it has some inherent shortcomings within the entire system. First, it is very subjective, as it depends entirely on the expertise of each particular dermatologist.

Large-Scale Systematic Reviews with Rigorous Assessment:
(K. Warin & S. Suebnukarn, 2024) employed the most comprehensive approach, systematically reviewing 54 qualified studies using PRISMA guidelines across three major databases (Medline, Google Scholar, Scopus) from 2000-2023. Their methodology included QUADAS-2 risk assessment, revealing 13 studies with low bias across all domains. Results showed exceptional performance: classification accuracy 85.0–100%, F1-scores 79.31–89.0%, Dice coefficient 76.0–96.3%, and Concordance index 0.78–0.95, with pooled diagnostic odds ratios of 2549.08 (95% CI 410.77–4687.39).

(Tapabrat Thakuria et al., 2024) focused specifically on handheld AI tools, searching PubMed, Scopus, Google Scholar, and Web of Science with PROSPERO registration. Their analysis of 25 papers using bubble and bar charts identified DenseNet121, VGG19, and EfficientNet-B0 as superior for binary classification, while EfficientNet-B4, Inception-V4, and Faster R-CNN excelled in multiclass tasks, achieving up to 100% precision, 99% specificity, and 97.5% accuracy.

Architecture-Specific Deep Learning Studies:
(R. O. Alabi et al., 2021) conducted targeted analysis of CNN-based prognostication across 34 studies using five databases (PubMed, Scopus, OvidMedline, Web of Science, IEEE). Their methodology distinguished between imaging modalities: spectra data achieved superior performance (average specificity 0.97, sensitivity 0.99, AUC 0.96, accuracy 96.6%) compared to CT images (specificity 0.84, sensitivity 0.81, AUC 0.967, accuracy 81.8%).

(C. Chu et al., 2021) employed comparative analysis between pathological and radiographic images for oral SCC diagnosis, prognosis, and metastasis detection. Results demonstrated pathological images' superiority (77.89–97.51% accuracy) over radiographic approaches (76–94.2% accuracy), with models successfully classifying cell types and predicting survival outcomes.

Multi-Modal and Hybrid Methodologies:
(Pankaj S. Shinde et al., 2025) presented the most technically diverse approach, analyzing CNNs, RNNs, autoencoders, extreme learning machines, Transformer models, and GANs across multiple imaging modalities including histopathology, hyperspectral imaging, autofluorescence, spectroscopy, and photography. However, specific performance metrics were not detailed in the abstract.

(Chen-xi Li et al., 2024) conducted meta-analysis of deep learning radiomics using ROBINS-I quality evaluation across 26 studies involving 64,731 medical images from 2012-2023. Their random-effects model yielded pooled sensitivity 0.88 (95%CI: 0.87∼0.88) and specificity 0.80 (95%CI: 0.80∼0.81), though Deeks' asymmetry test revealed slight publication bias (P = 0.03).

Specialized Technical Approaches:
(H. Mahmood et al., 2020) focused on whole slide image analysis for head and neck lesions, reviewing 11 studies with uni-centric datasets (40-270 images) using heuristics, supervised and unsupervised learning with over 10 classification techniques, achieving 79-100% accuracy despite high bias risk.

(R. O. Alabi et al., 2021a) examined 41 studies emphasizing support vector machines and artificial neural networks, reporting broad performance ranges: specificity 0.57-1.00, sensitivity 0.70-1.00, and accuracy 63.4-100.0%, highlighting significant methodological variability.

Emerging Technical Integration:
(M. Faisal et al., 2025) incorporated bio-inspired optimization techniques (PSOBER, Improved Whale Optimization Algorithm) alongside advanced models like CANet, InceptionResNetV2, EfficientNet, demonstrating the evolution toward hybrid AI approaches, though specific performance metrics weren't provided in the abstract.
